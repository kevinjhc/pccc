The Plaza honors the victims of sexual slavery during World War II and offers a quiet, welcoming space to everyone who seeks peace.

Statue of Peace by Seokyung Kim and Eunsung Kim, 2011

The Statue of Peace

A bronze girl sits with her fists resting on her knees. A small bird sits on her shoulder, and the chair beside her is empty. Visitors are invited to sit with her.

The sculpture is now in 17 places around the world, including 6 in the United States.

This statue commemorates hundreds of thousands of girls and women from Asia, Oceania, and Europe who fell victim to the systemic sexual slavery by the Japanese Imperial Army from 1931 to 1945. Starting in 1991, the survivors broke the cycle of shame and silence by testifying and urged the world to protect the future generations of women by facing the past and to build lasting peace.

Inscription

Our intent

  1. 01 — RememberGive voice to the victims of sexual violence and support peacemaking across communities.
  2. 02 — ReflectCreate a contemplative space for the forgotten faces of armed conflict, and invite visitors to help end inequality, violence, and enslavement.
  3. 03 — Come togetherTrust communities to see each other beyond national and cultural identities, and build lasting peace through open dialogue.

History

During World War II, more than 200,000 girls and women from 21 countries were coerced into sexual slavery by the Imperial Japanese Army. For decades their stories went untold.

  1. 1991Survivor Kim Haksoon gives her public testimony. Survivors around the world begin to come forward.
  2. 2011The Statue of Peace is created and installed in Seoul, South Korea.
  3. 2017The Committee enters Philadelphia's public art donation process, selects 805 S. Front Street, and begins outreach in Queen Village.
  4. 2019TEND landscape architects joins to design the plaza.
  5. 2021The Philadelphia Art Commission gives the project unanimous conceptual approval. The Committee becomes an independent organization.
  6. 2022The Committee presents at a City-hosted Town Hall and returns to the Art Commission for a second review.

A garden for quiet, shade, and gathering

Designed by TEND landscape architects with neighbors in Queen Village, who already call the site "the Sanctuary."

Programs

Free music, dance, poetry, and film that bring different communities together.

Opening Act

A small, mostly unamplified monthly concert series, May to October, with music from France, Korea, Latin America, and the Philippines.

AhnNyeong! Korea in Queen Village

Korean dance, music, and games for all ages each June during Immigrant Heritage Month.

The World After You Spoke

A poetry video by Philadelphia poets Michelle Meyer and Yolanda Wisher, marking 30 years since Kim Haksoon's testimony.
